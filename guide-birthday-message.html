How to write a birthday message that feels personal

Most birthday wishes sound the same because they are written for everyone. A message feels personal when it could only be sent to one person. Start with a specific memory instead of a general compliment: the trip where you missed the train, the night they stayed on the phone with you until sunrise, the way they laugh before the joke ends.

A good structure has three parts. First, a small moment you share. Second, what that moment taught you about them. Third, one honest wish for the year ahead. For example: "I still think about how you waited with me in the rain. That is who you are. I hope this year gives you people who wait for you the same way."

Keep it short enough to read in one breath, but not so short that it feels automatic. Three to six sentences is enough. Avoid listing every adjective you know; one true detail beats ten nice words.

The format matters too. A message tucked into a page with their favourite photo, a song they love and a countdown feels like a small event rather than a text they scroll past. That is the idea behind a personalised surprise page: the words stay the same, but the moment around them is yours.
